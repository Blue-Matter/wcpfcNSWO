2  Operating Models

2.1 OM Conditioning

The 2023 ISC stock assessment of North Pacific swordfish, which was fitted with Stock Synthesis 3 (SS3; Methot and Wetzel (2013)), was used to generate the operating models (OMs).

The assessment is a two-sex model with 4 quarterly time steps per year, covering 1975–2021. It includes 19 fishing fleets and 8 CPUE indices (Figure 2.1; Table 2.1). See the assessment report for further details on the assessment structure and assumptions.

The OMs were constructed by importing the SS3 output of each assessment run into the North Pacific swordfish MSE framework. The SS3 input files of the assessment runs are available from the WCNPOSWO-2023 repository.

The assessment runs were fitted with SS3 version 3.30.20.00, which has a bug in the calculation of spawning biomass. All runs were therefore re-run with SS3 version 3.30.23.1, using the original input files, and the OMs were constructed from the output of the re-run models. With the bug fixed, the unfished spawning biomass (\(SB_0\)) is calculated correctly; in the Base Case, \(SB_0\) is 105,070 t, compared with 99,808 t in the original assessment run.

2.1.1 Catch Units

In the assessment, the catch of 8 of the 19 fishing fleets is in numbers of fish (thousands), and the catch of the remaining fleets is in biomass (t). Because the total allowable catch (TAC) management advice produced by the candidate management procedures (CMPs) is in units of biomass (t), the catch of every fleet was imported in biomass, using the catch biomass predicted by SS3 for the fleets with catch in numbers.

2.1.2 Fleet Structure

To reduce run time, the 19 fishing fleets were combined into a single fleet in the operating models. The combined fleet reproduces the historical fishing mortality-at-age of the individual fleets, with selectivity, retention, and weight-at-age weighted by the fishing mortality of each fleet. This results in exactly the same fishery and population dynamics as an operating model with the 19 individual fleets. In the projection period, the relative contribution of each fleet (i.e., TAC allocation) is therefore assumed to be fixed at its recent pattern.

2.1.3 Indices of Abundance

Table 2.1 lists the CPUE indices in the assessment. The indices that continue to the terminal year are provided to the CMPs:

  • the late-phase Japanese longline indices for Area 1 and Area 2 (S2 and S4);
  • the Chinese Taipei distant-water longline index (S5);
  • the US shallow-set longline index (S8) (Figure 2.3; see Chapter 5).

The early-phase Japanese longline indices (S1 and S3) end in 1993 and are not used by the CMPs.

S5 and S8 were not fitted in the Base Case assessment (lambda = 0). However, fitting all indices was found to have little effect on the estimated stock status. Therefore S5 and S8 are provided to the CMPs, together with S2 and S4, so that CMPs can be developed that use these indices.

S6, an index of recruitment, is simulated in the projections and available to the CMPs, but it is not used by them. Using S6 in the CMPs was tested during the MSE development, but it did not improve their performance, largely because it does not track the adult abundance. The indices used by the CMPs are described in Section 5.3.

2.2 Base Case and Sensitivity OMs

The Base Case OM (OM 00) is constructed from the Base Case of the 2023 assessment described above, and the CMPs are tuned to this Base Case OM (see Section 5.6 for details on the CMP tuning).

The 10 sensitivity OMs included in the MSE are constructed in the same way from alternative SS3 runs, and are used to evaluate the robustness of the tuned CMPs to uncertainty in the stock dynamics that is not captured by the Base Case.

The sensitivity (robustness) OMs explore two sources of uncertainty:

  1. Life-history parameters: the sensitivity runs of the 2023 assessment with alternative natural mortality, steepness, growth, and maturity (runs 01–10; runs 05 and 06 are not included, see below).
  2. Increasing catchability: two runs that assume the catchability of the indices of abundance has increased over time (effort creep) by 1% (run 20a) and 2% (run 20b) per year. These scenarios follow those used in the IATTC MSE for bigeye tuna in the eastern Pacific Ocean, where annual increases in longline catchability of 0%, 1%, and 2% are used to represent uncertainty in effort creep (Xu and Maunder 2026). These runs are the Base Case fitted to the indices deflated by \((1 + c)^{y - y_0}\), where \(c\) is the annual effort creep and \(y_0\) the first year of each index. The OMs are conditioned on the deflated indices, but the CMPs use the observed indices, so the indices seen by the CMPs over-estimate the increase in abundance (or under-estimate the decline) in these OMs. The effort creep applies only to the historical period.

Table 2.2 lists the 12 life-history and catchability runs and their key parameters.

Two of these runs (05 with steepness 0.99 and 06 with maximum size 10% larger) are not included in the MSE. In these models the SS3 fits estimated an implausibly large stock, with ln(R0) of 32.37 and 35.06 compared with 6.84 in the Base Case, and unfished spawning biomass of the order of \(10^{16}\) and \(10^{17}\) t. The stock in these OMs is therefore effectively unfished and unaffected by the catch.

2.2.1 Effort Creep OMs

In the effort creep OMs (20a and 20b), SS3 is fitted to indices deflated by 1% and 2% per year, i.e. the indices that would be obtained if they were standardized for an assumed increase in catchability over the time series (Figure 2.9). The same increase in catchability is assumed for all indices.

The deflated indices are equal to the observed indices in the first year of each index, and decline relative to them over time, so the effort creep runs estimate a larger decline in abundance over the period of the indices. To explain this decline with the same catch history, the stock is estimated to have started from a much higher state: SB/SB0 in 1975 is 0.59 in run 20a and 0.98 in run 20b, compared with 0.3 in the Base Case (Figure 2.8). In 2021, SB/SB0 is 0.38 in the Base Case, and 0.32 and 0.28 in runs 20a and 20b.
