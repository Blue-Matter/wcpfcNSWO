5  Candidate Management Procedures

The MSE includes three types of candidate management procedures (CMPs):

  1. Index Rate (IR): an empirical (model-free) CMP that sets the TAC as a catch/index rate multiplied by the current values of the indices of abundance;
  2. Index Target (IT): an empirical, constant-catch CMP that sets the TAC at a tuned target catch, reduced when the indices fall below the threshold;
  3. Surplus Production (SP): a model-based CMP that fits a surplus production model to the catch and indices and sets the TAC from the estimated stock status and \(F_{\text{MSY}}\).

Each CMP type is combined with each of the 6 candidate harvest control rules (HCRs; Section 5.2) and 2 limits on the change in the TAC between management cycles (10% and 20%; Section 5.5), for a total of 36 CMPs (Table 5.1). All CMPs use the same 4 indices of abundance (S2, S4, S5, S8; Section 5.3).

During development, the CMPs were tested with four sets of the available indices: the Japanese late longline indices alone (S2, S4), the Japanese indices with the US shallow-set index (S2, S4, S8), the Japanese indices with the Taiwanese index (S2, S4, S5), and all 4 indices. The choice of index set made little difference to the performance of the CMPs in these runs. To avoid presenting 144 CMPs, the set with all 4 indices was used because it uses all the available information and makes the TAC less dependent on the CPUE series of any single fleet.

Each CMP is named by its type, HCR, and TAC change limit; e.g., IR_H2_10 is the Index Rate CMP with HCR 2 and a 10% limit on the change in the TAC.

All CMPs set a single annual TAC for the total catch (removals, in t) of all fleets, which is split among the fleets and quarters in proportion to their recent catch (Chapter 3). The CMPs use data aggregated to calendar years: annual catches (summed over the quarters) and annual indices (averaged over the quarters with data). The TAC is calculated every 3 years, with a 2-year data lag (Chapter 3), and is constant within each management cycle.

5.1 The Management Cycle

In each management cycle, the CMPs follow the same sequence of steps:

  1. Data: the TAC is calculated in the year before the management cycle starts, from the catch and index data up to 2 years before that (e.g., the data up to 2025 for the TAC calculated in 2027 for 2028–2030; Table 3.1).
  2. Stock status: the CMP estimates the current stock status, either from the indices relative to their level at \(SB_0\) (Index Rate and Index Target) or from the fitted surplus production model (Surplus Production).
  3. HCR: the estimated stock status determines the HCR multiplier, which reduces the fishing intensity (or catch) when the estimated status is below the threshold reference point (ThRP).
  4. Trial TAC: the multiplier is applied to a quantity specific to each CMP type (a catch/index rate, a reference catch, or \(F_{\text{MSY}}\)), scaled by the tuning parameter, to give a trial TAC.
  5. TAC change limit: the change from the previous TAC is limited to the TAC change limit of the CMP, and the TAC is left unchanged if the change would be less than 1%.

The steps differ among the CMP types, and are described in full in Section 5.4.1, Section 5.4.2, Section 5.4.3, and Section 5.5.

5.2 Harvest Control Rules

The candidate HCRs (Table 5.2) are defined in terms of stock status, \(SB/SB_0\), where \(SB_0\) is the equilibrium unfished spawning biomass, and fishing mortality (WCPFC 2026, Attachment 7, Table 2):

  • above the threshold reference point (ThRP), fishing mortality is at the target, \(F_{\text{target}}\);
  • between the ThRP and the limit reference point (LRP; 17% \(SB_0\)), fishing mortality decreases linearly from \(F_{\text{target}}\) to a minimum level, \(F_{\text{min}}\);
  • at and below the LRP, fishing mortality is \(F_{\text{min}}\).

\(F_{X\%}\) is the fishing mortality that reduces the spawning potential ratio to \(X\%\). \(F_{\text{min}}\) is \(F_{90\%}\) for all HCRs.

The HCRs are defined in terms of the true spawning biomass relative to \(SB_0\) and the true fishing mortality. None of these quantities are observed by the CMPs, which only have the catch and the indices of abundance. The CMPs therefore use the shape of each HCR, but the HCR is applied to estimated quantities and its output is not directly a fishing mortality.

Table 5.3 summarises how each element of the HCR is implemented in each CMP type.

The fishing mortality that results in the OM therefore depends on how well the estimated stock status (from the indices or the surplus production model) and the TAC match the true state of the stock, and can differ from the fishing mortality intended by the HCR.

5.2.1 HCR Multiplier

In all CMPs, the output of the HCR is a multiplier, \(\theta(x)\), calculated from the estimated stock status, \(x\):

\[ \theta(x) = \begin{cases} \phi & x \le \text{LRP} \\[4pt] \phi + (1 - \phi)\dfrac{x - \text{LRP}}{\text{ThRP} - \text{LRP}} & \text{LRP} < x < \text{ThRP} \\[4pt] 1 & x \ge \text{ThRP} \end{cases} \]

where \(\phi\) is the multiplier at and below the LRP:

  • Index Rate and Surplus Production: \(\phi = F_{\text{min}}/F_{\text{target}}\) of the HCR, the ratio of \(F_{\text{min}}\) to the F target in the per-recruit analysis of the Base Case OM;
  • Index Target: \(\phi = F_{\text{min}}/F_{\text{target}} \times \text{LRP}/\text{ThRP}\). The multiplier is lower because it applies to a catch rather than to a fishing intensity, and the catch at a given fishing intensity is lower when the stock is smaller.

For the index-based CMPs, \(x\) is the combined status of the indices (Section 5.3.4), and the LRP and ThRP are applied to it directly. For the Surplus Production CMPs, \(x\) is the estimated \(B/B_{\text{MSY}}\) of the surplus production model, and the LRP and ThRP are converted from \(B/K\), treated as equivalent to \(SB/SB_0\), to \(B/B_{\text{MSY}}\) by dividing them by \(B_{\text{MSY}}/K\) (0.5).

The HCR multiplier, as a function of estimated stock status, is shown for each HCR and CMP type in Figure 5.1.

5.3 Estimated Stock Status

5.3.1 Indices

All CMPs use the 4 indices of abundance that continue to the terminal year of the assessment:

  • the late-phase Japanese longline indices for Area 1 and Area 2 (S2 and S4);
  • the Chinese Taipei distant-water longline index (S5);
  • the US shallow-set longline index (S8).

S5 and S8 were not fitted in the Base Case assessment (lambda = 0), but are simulated in the projections with observation error conditioned on their fit to the OM, as are S2 and S4 (Chapter 3). In the index-based CMPs (Index Rate and Index Target), the indices are equally weighted (Section 5.3.4). In the Surplus Production CMPs, the indices have equal likelihood weights, but their influence on the fit depends on their estimated observation error (Section 5.4.3).

5.3.2 Index Level at \(SB_0\)

To apply the HCR control points, which are defined in units of \(SB/SB_0\), each index \(j\) is expressed relative to its level at \(SB_0\), \(I_{0,j}\).

\(I_{0,j}\) is calibrated on the Base Case OM:

\[ I_{0,j} = \operatorname*{median}_{s} \left( \frac{\bar{I}_{j,s}}{\overline{SB/SB_0}_{s}} \right) \]

where \(\bar{I}_{j,s}\) is the mean of the simulated index \(j\), including observation error, in simulation \(s\), and \(\overline{SB/SB_0}_{s}\) is the mean \(SB/SB_0\) in the same years. \(I_{0,j}\) is calculated once, from the Base Case OM, and used with every OM.

5.3.3 Current Status of Each Index

The current status of each index, \(S_j\), is the index averaged over the most recent RecentYears years of data (ignoring missing values), after optional loess smoothing. Both settings were selected for each index-based CMP type in Step 1 of the tuning procedure (see Section 5.6.1).

The selected values are:

  • Index Rate: RecentYears = 3 and Smooth = FALSE;
  • Index Target: RecentYears = 1 and Smooth = FALSE.

5.3.4 Combined Stock Status

Both index-based CMP types (Index Rate and Index Target) combine the indices into a single estimate of stock status, \(x\), as the mean of the status of each index relative to its level at \(SB_0\):

\[ x = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} \frac{S_j}{I_{0,j}} \]

where \(n\) is the number of indices (4).

5.4 Technical Description of CMPs

5.4.1 Index Rate

The Index Rate CMPs set the TAC as a catch/index rate multiplied by the current status of each index, in five steps:

  1. Calibration. Each index \(j\) is calibrated into its own catch/index rate, \(\hat{r}_j\): the mean total catch over the last 2 historical years divided by the mean of index \(j\) over the same years. The calibration is done once, from the historical data (2020–2021), and is the same in every management cycle: \[ \hat{r}_j = \frac{\bar{C}_{\text{hist}}}{\bar{I}_{\text{hist},j}} \]
  2. Stock status. The current status of each index, \(S_j\), and the estimated stock status, \(x\), are calculated as described in Section 5.3.
  3. HCR. The HCR multiplier, \(\theta(x)\), is calculated from the estimated stock status (Section 5.2.1).
  4. Trial TAC. Each index’s catch/index rate is scaled by the tuning parameter, \(m\), and the HCR multiplier, and multiplied by the current status of that index, giving the TAC implied by each index. The trial TAC is the mean over the indices: \[ \text{TrialTAC} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n} S_j \times m \times \hat{r}_j \times \theta(x) \] Because the catch/index rate is calibrated to the recent catch, \(m\) = 1 with \(\theta\) = 1 sets a TAC equal to the calibration catch when the indices are at their calibration level.
  5. TAC change limit. The TAC change limit is applied (Section 5.5).

The fishing intensity of the Index Rate CMPs is therefore constant (apart from the HCR) and the TAC follows the indices: an increase in the indices increases the TAC in proportion.

5.4.2 Index Target

The Index Target CMPs set the TAC as a target catch (the reference catch scaled by the tuning parameter), reduced when the estimated stock status is below the ThRP, in five steps:

  1. Reference catch. The reference catch, \(\bar{C}\), is the mean total catch over the last 5 historical years (2017–2021), and is the same in every management cycle.
  2. Stock status. The current status of each index, \(S_j\), and the estimated stock status, \(x\), are calculated as described in Section 5.3.
  3. HCR. The HCR multiplier, \(\theta(x)\), is calculated from the estimated stock status, with the \(\phi\) of the Index Target CMPs (Section 5.2.1).
  4. Trial TAC. The trial TAC is the reference catch, scaled by the tuning parameter, \(m\), and the HCR multiplier: \[ \text{TrialTAC} = m \times \bar{C} \times \theta(x) \]
  5. TAC change limit. The TAC change limit is applied (Section 5.5).

The Index Target CMPs are therefore a constant-catch type of CMP. When the estimated stock status is above the ThRP, the trial TAC does not depend on the level of the indices: it is a fixed target catch, \(m \times \bar{C}\), which is also the maximum TAC of the CMP. The TAC moves to the target catch, within the TAC change limit, and then stays there.

The indices are used only to reduce the TAC when the estimated stock status falls below the ThRP. Because \(m\) is tuned, the target catch is not the same as the reference catch: the tuned \(m\) of the Index Target CMPs is 1.14–1.39, so their target catch is 14%–39% above \(\bar{C}\).

In contrast, the TACs of the Index Rate and Surplus Production CMPs follow the indices or the estimated biomass at all levels of stock status.

5.4.3 Surplus Production

The Surplus Production CMPs fit a Schaefer surplus production model to the annual catch and the 4 indices.

The estimated parameters are \(F_{\text{MSY}}\), MSY, and the initial depletion (\(B/K\)) at the start of the first fitted year. The carrying capacity, \(K\), follows from \(F_{\text{MSY}}\) and MSY (\(K = 2\,\text{MSY}/F_{\text{MSY}}\) for the Schaefer model), and the biomass in each later year is calculated from the initial depletion, \(K\), the productivity, and the observed catches. The biomass in the last data year is projected to the start of the management cycle (see below), and the resulting \(B/B_{\text{MSY}}\) is the estimated stock status used in the HCR.

The catch and indices contain little information on the size of the stock as the catch has been nearly constant since 1975, and the indices have no strong trend or contrast. With biomass fixed at \(K\) at the start of the catch series, the estimates of MSY and \(K\) ran to their upper bounds and most fits failed. The model is therefore:

  • fitted from 1994, the start of the late-phase Japanese longline indices (the early-phase indices, S1 and S3, end in 1993 and are not used), to the last data year;
  • fitted with the depletion in 1994 estimated (starting value 0.5);
  • fitted with weakly informative lognormal priors on \(F_{\text{MSY}}\) (median 0.2, CV 2), depletion in 1994 (median 0.5, CV 0.5), and MSY (median 25,737 t, twice the mean historical catch, CV 1).

The Surplus Production CMPs set the TAC from the estimated \(F_{\text{MSY}}\) and stock status, in five steps:

  1. Model fit. The model is fitted to the catch and index data available in the management cycle. Each fit starts from the estimates of the previous management cycle. If the fit fails or does not converge, the previous TAC is kept.
  2. Stock status. The fitted model is projected from the last data year to the start of the first year of the management cycle, with the catch in the intervening years equal to the TAC in force. The estimated stock status, \(x = \hat{B}/\hat{B}_{\text{MSY}}\), is the projected biomass relative to \(\hat{B}_{\text{MSY}}\) at the start of the management cycle.
  3. HCR. The HCR multiplier, \(\theta(x)\), is calculated from the estimated stock status, with the control points converted to \(B/B_{\text{MSY}}\) (Section 5.2.1).
  4. Trial TAC. The fishing mortality is the estimated \(F_{\text{MSY}}\), scaled by the tuning parameter, \(m\), and the HCR multiplier: \[ F = m \times \hat{F}_{\text{MSY}} \times \theta(x) \] The model is projected at \(F\) for the 3 years of the management cycle, and the trial TAC is the mean annual catch of this projection.
  5. TAC change limit. The TAC change limit is applied (Section 5.5).

The fishing mortality of the Surplus Production CMPs is therefore constant relative to the estimated \(F_{\text{MSY}}\) (apart from the HCR), and the TAC follows the estimated biomass.

In the projections, the surplus production model converged in at least 96.9% of the model fits (simulations × management cycles) for every Surplus Production CMP and OM.

5.5 TAC Change Limits

For all CMPs, the proportional change from the previous TAC is limited to 10% or 20% (the TAC change limit of each CMP), both up and down, and changes smaller than 1% leave the TAC unchanged. With \(R = \text{TrialTAC}/\text{TAC}_{\text{prev}}\), the ratio of the trial TAC to the previous TAC:

\[ \text{TAC} = \text{TAC}_{\text{prev}} \times \begin{cases} 1 & \lvert R - 1 \rvert < 0.01 \\[4pt] 1 - \delta & R < 1 - \delta \\[4pt] 1 + \delta & R > 1 + \delta \\[4pt] R & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

where \(\delta\) is the TAC change limit and \(\text{TAC}_{\text{prev}}\) is the TAC in force. In the first management cycle (2028), \(\text{TAC}_{\text{prev}}\) is the total catch in the last data year.

5.6 Tuning Methodology

Each CMP has a single tuning parameter, \(m\), that scales the quantity to which the HCR multiplier is applied: the catch/index rate (Index Rate), the reference catch (Index Target), or \(F\) relative to \(\hat{F}_{\text{MSY}}\) (Surplus Production).

Each CMP is tuned in the Base Case OM to maximise the mean annual catch over 2028–2057, subject to the Safety and Status objectives (Chapter 4):

  • Safety: the probability that spawning biomass is above the LRP (17% \(SB_0\)) in any given year is at least 0.8;
  • Status: the probability that fishing mortality is at or below the \(F_{\text{target}}\) of the HCR of the CMP (\(F_{40\%}\), \(F_{35\%}\), or \(F_{30\%}\)) is at least 0.5.

Both probabilities are calculated from the true spawning biomass and fishing mortality in the OM, over all simulations and years.

The tuning is done in two steps.

5.6.1 Step 1: CMP Settings

The settings of the index-based CMPs that are not set by the HCR or the tuning parameter were selected from a set of candidate values (Table 5.4), using one CMP of each type: HCR 2 and a 10% TAC change limit.

Each configuration was projected at 5 values of \(m\) over 0.25–4, using the first 50 simulations, and the catch at the largest value of \(m\) that meets both constraints was interpolated. The 2 configurations with the highest catch were then fully tuned (Step 2) on all simulations, and the configuration with the highest catch was selected. The selected settings were then used for all CMPs of that type.

5.6.2 Step 2: Tuning Parameter

\(m\) was then tuned for each CMP. It was first evaluated on a grid of 7 log-spaced values over 0.25–4, which was extended if necessary, and then refined towards the largest value that meets both constraints (or the value with the highest catch, if that is lower), until the range of \(m\) is within 1% or the binding constraint is within 0.005 of its minimum.

5.6.3 Tuning Results

All 36 CMPs were tuned successfully. For 35 CMPs, the Status constraint determined the tuned value of \(m\) (Status 0.498–0.523), and for one CMP (IR_H6_20) the mean catch reached its maximum before either constraint was binding.

Safety was not limiting for any CMP: the probability that SB > LRP was at least 0.83 for every CMP, above the minimum of 0.8.

Because the Status constraint determines the tuning, the tuned catch depends mainly on the F target of the HCR (Figure 5.2; Table 5.5). The HCRs with the same F target but different ThRPs give similar results because, in the Base Case OM, the stock is above the ThRP in most years when the CMPs are tuned to the F target (65%–99% of years, depending on the CMP), so the HCR ramp has little effect (Table 5.6).
